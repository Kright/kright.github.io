*Updated 2026-10-31: the first version said MTP doesn't work over RPC. It does, one flag was missing.*

I have two 16 GB GPUs at home, but in different computers: an RTX 4080 in the desktop and an RTX 5060 Ti in the home server. Gemma 4 31B in a decent 4-bit quant weighs 17 GB and doesn't fit into either of them. You could take iq3, but then quality suffers, and you still can't get a big context.

But llama.cpp has RPC: on one machine you run `rpc-server`, which simply serves up its GPU over the network, and on the other - a regular `llama-server`, which sees the remote card as just another device and spreads the model's layers across them. So that's what I tried.

## How it works


- The desktop with the 4080 is the coordinator. It runs `llama-server`, and the model can be taken straight from the LM Studio folder.
- The server with the 5060 Ti is the worker. It runs `rpc-server` 24/7, and when idle it takes up 180 MB of VRAM.
- Both are in docker, and the image has to be built by hand: the official llama.cpp images are built without RPC. And the image has to be byte-for-byte identical on both machines, so it's built once and carried over with `docker save | docker load`.

The model is `gemma-4-31B-it-qat-UD-Q4_K_XL` from unsloth. It's QAT, meaning the model was fine-tuned with quantization in mind, and 4 bits cost it almost nothing. The weights are split roughly in half, the remote card goes first and the local one last - it holds the output layer, so the logits don't have to travel over the network.

## What came out of it

26 tokens per second on short requests. 64K context, and 4 parallel requests still fit on top of that: 23 tok/s each, about 90 in total.

The network barely gets in the way. Everything runs into GPU memory: every token means reading all 17 GB of weights, and the 5060 Ti's memory is one and a half times slower than the 4080's. Plus the cards work in turns, first one computes its layers, then the other. The theoretical ceiling from memory bandwidth is about 32 tok/s, so 26 is perfectly fair.

And then came the surprises.

**MTP works, but there's a catch.** On a single GPU it gave me a 2x speedup. Here the speed didn't grow at all at first, and on a long context it dropped by half: 7 tok/s versus 14. Every step with MTP cost almost as much as three regular tokens, and the longer the context, the worse it got. I went through the flags, nothing helped, I turned it off and wrote in the first version of this post that it doesn't work over RPC.

One flag was missing: `--spec-draft-device CUDA0`. Without it the small MTP model is spread across the cards the same way as the main one, and a piece of it ends up on the remote card. But on every step it needs the main model's hidden states from the local card, so those went back and forth over the network. Its guesses were fine all along, so the logs showed nothing suspicious. With the flag everything sits on the 4080 next to the output layer, and you get 60-80 tok/s versus 26, and 64 versus 21 at 10K context. Draft depth 4 works best. Four parallel requests add up to about 140 tokens per second.

**KV cache in f16 is faster than in q8.** Out of habit I set q8_0: half the memory, and therefore, I thought, faster too. But at 10K context f16 gives 23.5 tok/s versus 20. Apparently unpacking q8 is more expensive than reading twice as much. Though with 128K context f16 no longer fits, so I went with 64K and f16.

Another nice little thing: `llama-server` has `--sleep-idle-seconds`. When idle it unloads the model from both cards and loads it back on its own on the first request. The first reply after sleep arrives in about 45 seconds, but in exchange the VRAM isn't occupied when it isn't needed.

## Wrapping up

- Two GPUs in different computers do add up into one through llama.cpp RPC, and a 31B model in 4 bits runs on them at a decent speed.
- Parallel requests are almost free: four at once go at almost the same speed as one.
- MTP over RPC gives a 2.5-3x speedup, but the small model has to be explicitly placed on the same card as the output layer. Otherwise it doesn't speed things up, it quietly slows them down.
- A quantized KV cache saves memory, but can slow down generation. Worth measuring instead of setting q8 out of habit.
